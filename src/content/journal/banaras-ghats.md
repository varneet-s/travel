Banaras does not operate on ordinary time. At 5:30 in the morning, when fog still hovers over the quiet expanse of the Ganga, the city wakes to the sound of temple bells and the rhythmic creak of wooden oars dipping into water.

Rowing past the ancient stone ramparts of the ghats, centuries of history rise up directly from the riverbank. Every ghat tells a different story—Assi where priests chant morning mantras, Chet Singh Fort with its imposing battlements, and Manikarnika where the eternal flames never extinguish.

In the narrow alleys behind the river, motorbikes cannot pass. You walk past centuries-old wooden doorframes, hidden chai stalls serving cardamom tea in clay kulhads, and small rooftop terraces that overlook the infinite sea of old city roofs. At night, placing a flickering diya on the gentle river current, you feel connected to millions of travelers who have stood on these exact stone steps across thousands of years.
