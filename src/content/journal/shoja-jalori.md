Shoja sits five kilometers below Jalori Pass, perched on a steep hillside covered in dense cedar and spruce. Unlike Jibhi below, Shoja rarely gets crowded. Sound here travels far: the occasional horn of an HRTC bus grinding up in first gear, the ring of temple bells in the morning mist, and wind through the treetops.

The walk up to Jalori and onward to Serolsar Lake leads through old-growth oak and kharsu forests. The trail opens up into quiet clearings where local shepherds graze their sheep under the shadow of Ragupur Fort. 

Even in mid-May, the air stays sharp. Nights are spent with wool blankets, listening to rain drumming against corrugated tin roofs.
