After five intense days in Banaras, I took a train to Lucknow for two solitary days. Solo travel in Awadh has an almost poetic intimacy. Without a group itinerary, I spent hours wandering through old Chowk and Hazratganj with a pocket notebook.

Standing on the vast sunlit stone terrace of the Bara Imambara, the colossal Mughal-Awadhi arches frame the Lucknow skyline in timeless symmetry. The labyrinthine passages of the Bhool Bhulaiya above the main hall whisper with acoustic tricks—footsteps echoing three corridors away.

In the late afternoon, I stood beneath the lotus-carved stone pillars framing the historic Husainabad clock tower. The golden winter sun washed over the pale stucco walls, casting long shadows across quiet courtyards. Detouring alone was a reminder that some cities are best experienced in quiet footsteps.
