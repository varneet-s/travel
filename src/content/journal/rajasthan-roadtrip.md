Fourteen continuous days on the road across Rajasthan. Three friends, one car, and an endless ribbon of asphalt stretching between the blue walls of Jodhpur, the golden ramparts of Jaisalmer, and the quiet waters of Udaipur.

Watching the sunset from the towering cliffs of Mehrangarh Fort was mesmerizing. Below, the maze of indigo rooftops reflected the fading twilight while eagles circled on desert thermals. Further west towards the Thar, golden sandstone chhatri pavilions stood solitary against vast horizons, their delicate jali stone screens catching amber light.

Long road trips build their own rhythm: early morning dhabas serving piping hot pyaz kachoris, desert wind whipping through open windows on state highways, and twilight stillness by quiet lakes as flocks of migratory birds settle into the reeds. Two weeks of open road reminded me that distance measured in friendships is the best kind of travel.
