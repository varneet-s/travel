In January 2024, Sahaj and I packed minimal layers and drove north into deep winter. Old Manali was quiet—wooden chalets framed by bare apple branches and narrow stone lanes slick with black ice.

The turning point was entering the southern portal of the Atal Tunnel. You drive through nine kilometers of subterranean concrete darkness, and emerge into the breathtaking, desolate expanse of Lahaul. 

The contrast was shocking: gone were the dense green pine forests of Kullu. Instead, Sissu lay draped in pure, untouched white snow beneath jagged pyramidal peaks. The Chandra river flowed under sheets of ice, and frozen cascades hung like giant organ pipes from cliff faces. Riding along the empty high-altitude highway in sub-zero air was cold enough to numb fingers, but the absolute stillness of Lahaul in winter is something you never forget.
