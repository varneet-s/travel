The road into Kinnaur clings to sheer vertical gneiss walls carved out by the Baspa River. By the time you reach Chitkul at 3,450 meters, the gorge widens into a high glacial valley flanked by snow-capped peaks that mark the frontier.

In June, the air still carries a sharp, high-altitude chill. Walking past the Mathi Temple in the center of the village, you notice the centuries-old Kathkuni wood-and-stone construction, intricate animal carvings, and mountain dogs sleeping in the courtyard stone flags. 

Down by the gravel banks of the Baspa, glacier melt runs an unreal turquoise against white river boulders. We sat on the riverbank for hours, watching clouds drift over the glaciated cirques. There is an unmistakable sense of culmination here—the feeling that the paved road has finally given way to wilderness.
